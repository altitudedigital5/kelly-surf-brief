Why mornings are offshore.

Overnight the land cools faster than the ocean, so cooler air drains downhill and out to sea as a light E breeze. Blowing into the wave face, it holds the lip up and grooms the surface, which makes waves cleaner but also steeper and hollower. As the sun heats the land, the flow flips onshore, which is why the clean window closes by late morning.

Skip

The swampy high tide from about 8:30 to 10:30a everywhere. Little Point (over its 5.5 ft tide ceiling all morning, then falling). Birdrock (W swell blocked). The SW-wind afternoon after about 1p.

Gotchas

Size: SRF 3 to 5 ft with sets to 6 on mixed 210° and 280° swell, and the buoy groundswell is 3.0 to 3.9 ft at 13–14s, so the average waves are in your range but the sets are near your ceiling on the Anvil. Offshore wind holds the faces up, so the sets will feel steeper and pitch harder; let the biggest ones go. HIGH rip current risk (NWS SRF). The La Jolla gauge is still running about 1.2 ft over predictions, so the beach gets narrow and backwashy as the high fills. Blacks: hike, shorebreak, canyon crowds.
