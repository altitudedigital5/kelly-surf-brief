Groundswell vs windsea.

The buoy splits the ocean into two parts. Groundswell (14s today) was made by a far-off storm and arrives as organized lines. Windsea (5s today) is being made right here by local wind, so it is short, steep and lumpy and rides on top of the swell. When the windsea is as big as the swell, the faces get bumpy and the sets break unpredictably, which is why 5 ft at 14s can still be a bad day.

Skip

The swampy high tide from about 8:30a to noon everywhere. Little Point (hits its 5.5 ft real-tide ceiling by about 7a). PB Point, Tourmaline and Birdrock (tide too high). The SW-wind afternoon and the High Surf Advisory from 4p.

Gotchas

Size: SRF 4 to 6 ft with sets to 7, and the buoy reads 4.9 ft of 14s W groundswell plus 4–5 ft of short-period windsea, so this is above your ceiling on the Anvil and lumpy rather than clean. HIGH rip current risk and a High Surf Advisory from 4p today to 10a Sunday (NWS). Thunderstorms are in the morning forecast: get out at the first thunder or lightning. Rain also starts the county 72-hour runoff clock, so stay away from river mouths and storm drains. The gauge is running about 1.4 ft over predictions, so the beach gets narrow and backwashy as the high fills.
